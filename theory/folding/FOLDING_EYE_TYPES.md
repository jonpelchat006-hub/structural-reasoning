# Different Cuts, Different Cones, Different Eyes — the cone recurs at every scale

*Companion to `FOLDING_FUNNEL.md`, `FOLDING_THE_EYE.md`, `FOLDING_TUNING_SHELLS.md`, `FOLDING_RECURSIVE_C.md`. Jonathan: different cuts might make different cones — is that how the different kinds of eyes work? Tested; `cones_test.py`.*

## Different cuts DO make different cones (CONFIRMED, molecular scale)
Opening half-angle across real active-site pockets (ray-cast from the ligand apex): 1AKE (AP5A) **0° (sealed apex)**, 1B8A (ATP) 27°, 2YHX 42°, 1GD1 (NAD) 45°, 1PHP (ADP) 55°, 2CTS (CoA) 58°. A real spread — pinhole-tight to a wide 58° cone. Same convergence, cut differently, different aperture.

## Eyes are the premier "one function, many geometries" (established)
Eyes evolved independently ~40+ times; **eight major optical types** (Land & Nilsson), each a different way of shaping the light-cone: pit, pinhole, camera/lens, concave mirror (single-chamber); apposition, superposition (compound). The governing law is a **cone-width tradeoff** — narrow → acuity/little light, wide → sensitivity/blur — exactly "different cones."

## Honest boundary — a scale relationship, not a protein cut building an eye
Eye types are set by organ-level optics (lens/mirror/aperture), not by how a single protein's domain is cut. What makes the instinct right anyway: **the cone recurs at every scale** — molecular (opsin pocket funnel, 0–58°), cellular (photoreceptor acceptance cone), organ (the whole eye funneling to the retina). Three nested cones, each tunable narrow-or-wide — the recursion theme applied to vision: the shape of the convergence sets the reading behavior, at every scale.
