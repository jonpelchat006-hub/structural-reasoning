# Does life glow? — the color/spray idea meets real photophysics

*Companion to `FOLDING_VORTEX.md` ("reading = what the turn emits at closure"). Jonathan: life uses color more than we think — humans give off a low glow, biolum/chemilum are real, "we are reading the product or the losses," colors have a readable frequency. Checked against the literature (photophysics, not a structural test).*

## What is real (established)
- **Ultraweak photon emission (UPE / "biophotons"):** tissue emits ~1–1000 photons/cm²/s across ~200–750 nm — extraordinarily faint but measurable above thermal background. The human body glows on a **diurnal rhythm** (Kobayashi 2009); a 2025 study reports it largely stops at death.
- **Mechanism = reading the losses (literally):** oxidative chemistry (ROS, peroxidase/lipoxygenase) creates triplet excited species; when they relax they shed the energy as a photon. The light *is* the energy loss radiated.
- **Frequency = the energy step (E = hν):** color encodes the size of the drop; UPE is read as an oxidative-stress readout, the band identifying the species (excited carbonyls ≈ blue-green, singlet oxygen ≈ red). "Colors have a frequency that can be read" is exactly right.
- **Bioluminescence** (luciferin/luciferase) and **chemiluminescence** are fully established; biolum *is* functional signaling between organisms.

## The honest boundary (contested)
Split two claims: (1) the glow exists and *we* can read it — solid; (2) the organism reads its *own* glow internally as a signal channel — **not established, actively contested** (the emission is orders of magnitude below ambient light; no intracellular detector shown). Tie-in: for oxidoreductases (the set has GAPDH/LDH), the closure emission can literally be a photon whose color is the energy released — the confirmed piece (reactions write energy into a frequency); the open piece is whether that color is read back as the system's information channel.

*Sources: Biophoton (Wikipedia); Kobayashi et al., PLOS ONE 4:e6256 (2009); phys.org (2025).*
