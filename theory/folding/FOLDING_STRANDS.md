# The Wire-Strand Model — bundled core, single loops clumping around ATP

*Companion to `FOLDING_ATP_REFERENCE.md`, `FOLDING_KNIT_WEAVE.md`. Jonathan's image: the backbone is like a multi-strand electrical wire — bundled in the core, branching to reach substructures, and at the borders single strands again, clumping around ATP, with uneven counts across the windows. Tested on 10 ligand-bound enzymes; `strands.py`.*

## Results
1. **UNBUNDLING (CONFIRMED):** ligand-contacting residues are **68% coil vs 52%** for the whole chain — enriched in all 10. The bundled regular structure unravels into single loops right where the chain touches the ligand.
2. **but they CLUMP (CONFIRMED, nuance):** local packing at the ligand region 9.4 vs core 8.6 — not looser, if anything denser. Unbundled in *conformation* (loops), bundled in *space* (dense cluster) — exactly "single strands clumping around ATP."
3. **ASYMMETRY (CONFIRMED):** distinct loop segments reaching the ligand split ~**5.9 : 1** across the two domains; several all from one domain (open/single-cleft states), the bridged/closed ones more balanced (1GD1 4|5) — ties to the open/closed mechanism.

## Status
A **description**, and a good one — the wire-strand picture matches the ligand environment across 10 diverse enzymes (unravel, clump, converge asymmetrically), consistent with the loop-lined-active-site principle and induced fit. Not a predictor (none of these features beat guessing the center, `FOLDING_SITE_PREDICTION.md`). Describes well; does not predict.
