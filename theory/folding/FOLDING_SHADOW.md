# The Shadow — the net ±1 is a HEIGHT, and a single-angle reader can't see it

*Companion to `FOLDING_CONTINUITY.md`, `SPHERE_GENERATOR.md` Part B. Jonathan: could the net ±1 be ending up at a different HEIGHT? and the "big wire" only sees one angle, so a height change doesn't show up — the shadow leaves the same mark. Tested; `shadow_test.py`.*

## 1 — the ±1 is literally a height gap (CONFIRMED)
Signed terminus heights along the cut normal: **net ±1 ⟺ termini at opposite heights** (6LDH +50/−2.9 → −1; 1CLL −11.9/+10.9 → +1); net 0 ⟺ same side (1GD1 +18.8/+16.9). The ±1 is exactly "ending up at a different height."

## 2 — the conservation is angle-proof; the ±1 is not (CONFIRMED — the shadow effect)
Sweeping 400 random projection axes: **max|net| = 1 from EVERY angle, all 8** (the continuity law is projection-independent — the invariant). But **whether you SEE the ±1 depends on the angle**: net-±1 proteins read net 0 from ~35–40% of directions (1CLL 40%, 6LDH 35%) — the height offset vanishes and they look like the net-0 group. Total crossings swing wildly with angle (8TLN 1–19). Only the net is stable.

## Honest correction
6LDH/8TLN don't "fail to close" — they DO close, just at a different height, and to a reader that sees only the axial shadow that height is invisible and free. Whether the ±1 costs anything depends on the reader: a **projective** (1D shadow) reader — height hidden, ±1 free; a **full-3D** reader — the height offset is real. This is the ray-vs-ribbon point made quantitative: a 1D reader gets only "where it goes," blind to height/twist. The net is the one quantity no viewing angle can distort.
