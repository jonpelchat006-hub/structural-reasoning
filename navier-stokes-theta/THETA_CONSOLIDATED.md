# A load-field interface for incompressible Navier–Stokes: what four days of numerics established, falsified, and left open

*Consolidated from the navier_stokes_theta folder, 13–16 September 2026. Every number below has a source file named in INDEX.md; nothing here is a claim about the continuum equations.*

## 1. The question

Shovelcat Theory (Theory 1, Oct 2025) defines a dimensionless load field theta with three factors,

    theta = (1 + w_S ln r_S^{p_S}) (1 + w_T sqrt(ln r_T^{p_T})) (1 + w_E sqrt(ln r_E^{p_E})),
    (w_S, w_T, w_E) = (0.8, 0.6, 0.4),  (p_S, p_T, p_E) = (1.2, 1.0, 0.8)   [Table 1, p. 37],

an Euler–Lagrange equation of reaction–diffusion type for it, and an interface Gamma that nucleates where theta = pi, on which theta is pinned and across which a flux J = [[kappa d_n theta]] balances — a sink that "extracts perturbation energy and damps chaos". The folder's question was whether this structure says anything about the Navier–Stokes regularity problem, and whether the mechanism, translated faithfully, could be a regularizer.

## 2. Setup common to all runs

Dimensionless incompressible Navier–Stokes on a periodic cube of side 2 pi; Fourier pseudo-spectral, rotational form, strict 2/3 dealiasing, RK4; 24^3–96^3; nu = .03 to .007. Two flow families: a powered ring of eight rotational drives with an advected thermal field (viscous heating, insulated or Newton-cooled), and freely decaying antiparallel Gaussian vortex tubes with a Kerr/Crow-type perturbation. Every run reports an energy budget (kinetic + heat + removed heat + sink = input or initial energy), a CFL ceiling, and at least two grids or a dt halving where a number is quoted. Budget closures are 1e-8 or better throughout.

## 3. Results, in the order they forced each other

**3.1 The published theta cannot be a regularity mechanism.** With a fixed baseline reference and one-way thermal coupling, theta is a monotone relabeling of the temperature field (THETA_FIELD). With the paper's own mean-of-surroundings reference, theta reaches pi — but only through the entropy factor (peak 4.8; the temperature factor tops out at 1.14 and the kinetic factor at 1.6–1.9 by the weights). The entropy factor measures a *contrast* between a hot spot and its surroundings. In decaying tubes with initial energy 0.15 -> 0.3 -> 0.6 the peak gradient rises 9.8 -> 22.4 and the interface *weakens*: lifetime 1.2 -> 0.4 -> none (THETA_TUBES). More energetic flows heat the whole box faster, and the contrast collapses while the gradients grow. A local reference (l = .5, 1.0) makes it worse; cooling restores formation but not the trend. The forced ring's earlier cubic scaling of the interface flux came from the drive holding a steady hot spot, not from gradients. Conclusion: with Table-1 weights the mechanism strengthens only where a persistent source keeps a contrast alive — the planetary setting of the paper — and weakens in the blow-up setting. "Always wins" is falsified for the published form.

**3.2 The two kinetic planes do track sharpening.** The velocity gradient A = S + W supplies two orthogonal load ratios by the same recipe: on the magnitude plane r_E = (w.S.w)^+ / (nu |grad w|^2) (enstrophy production over destruction), and on the direction plane r_R = |(I - xi xi^T) S xi| / (nu |grad xi|^2) (strain tilting the vorticity direction over viscous smoothing of it; Constantin–Fefferman's quantity). Across the same E0 sweep r_E rises 16.5 -> 24 and r_R 3.4 -> 5.3 at the 99th percentile. A theta that strengthens with gradients must be carried by these planes; with the entropy factor's weight (w p = 0.96, no root) on the magnitude plane the two-plane product reaches 5.6–6.9.

**3.3 The geometry of the load, measured.** In the top-1% gradient cells: the vorticity direction locks onto a strain axis (alignment .85–.93, 60–77% fully locked, vs .57 and ~20% in the bulk); the frame rotation rate drops to ~.1 of the strain rate while the bulk's rises with E0 (.17 -> .30); the pressure-Hessian frame is orthogonal to the strain frame (cos .02–.11 vs .56–.71 in the bulk); the vorticity term (W^2) counter-rotates the strain frame against the pressure Hessian and cancels a large part of its rotation (inert in the bulk); and tan(phi) = tilt/stretch of the vorticity direction is .36–.62 at the load and 1.1–1.5 in the bulk. This is Jonathan's picture — two gyroscopic planes that lock into octants (walls) under load, a third ring anchoring them, something at an angle (a right angle) — and it is the geometric depletion of nonlinearity the Constantin–Fefferman theorem is about. But the geometry *characterizes* the load without *locating* it: a criterion built from these four numbers overlaps the load with Jaccard <= .2 (locked, orthogonal, anchored cells occur in many benign places at low intensity), whereas r_E alone reaches .3–.5, and gating r_E by alignment makes it worse. The interface must be nucleated by the magnitude plane; the geometry may gate the sink.

**3.4 The two orthogonal planes are the fluid's own.** The first is the Helmholtz split: solenoidal velocity (the executed path) versus the gradient subspace removed by projection — the pressure head Pi, which does no net work, is enforced instantaneously, and reads zero to the dynamics. Locally the exchange j = -u.grad Pi = -div(Pi u) is the pointwise hand-off between the planes, and the interface sits at the top ~1% of |j| with a definite positive sign (the pressure plane delivering into the normal plane). The second is the strain eigenframe (3.3). The velocity-gradient invariants (Q, R) close the picture: the restricted-Euler closure with an isotropic pressure Hessian blows up along D = 27R^2/4 + Q^3 -> 0, R > 0 (Vieillefosse); the loaded cells in every run have D > 0 (vortical) 100% of the time, never on that tail, and the measured pressure–strain orthogonality is precisely the Hessian anisotropy that keeps them off it. Quaternions are the natural representation for the frame, its rates and the Q–R plane; the theory's "closure equation" has its physical counterpart in the pressure-Hessian closure problem.

**3.5 Form D: an interface that can be built.** theta_s = 1 + 0.96 ln r_E, clipped to [-pi, pi]; a restoring wall beyond |theta| = 3; a relax–diffuse–advect field equation; Gamma = {theta >= 3}; on Gamma a sink removing power kappa a j^+ (a = alignment gate), booked in the budget. Two things the dynamics forced: the field's response time tau must beat the load's residence time at any place (~h/U ~ 0.06): with tau = .1 no interface ever forms, with tau = .02 it forms on the load (35–54% of Gamma inside the top-1% gradient cells). And Gamma = {theta >= pi} is unreachable once the source is clipped at pi and smoothed; the wall onset at 3 is the usable threshold. Results (E0 = .6): sink share .9–1.6% of E0 rising with load; peak vorticity -6.5%; draining Gamma raises the pressure-plane delivery into it (7.7 -> 9.4), a positive feedback on the sink.

**3.6 Reynolds-number trend.** 64^3 at nu = .02, .01 and 96^3 at nu = .007 (Re ~ 100–300, an enstrophy-growing regime at nu <= .01): the sink's effect on peak enstrophy is -0.3%, -4.8%, -6.2%, growing with Re and, at fixed nu, growing through time as the load builds (0.3% -> 6.2% over the nu = .007 run). The loaded cells stay vortical. The failure mode: Gamma dilutes, from 0.11% to 0.23% of the volume, and its overlap with the top-1% gradient region falls from ~.4 to ~.1 — with a fixed 0.96 weight the field saturates over a broad band at higher Re. A thin-interface rule that scales with the load is the open modelling question.

**3.7 Two-sided interface.** Sending the sink energy into a reservoir with decay time .2 and returning a fraction beta_b to the flow on Gamma: at full return the enstrophy suppression is mostly lost (-4.8% -> -1.4%) but the peak-vorticity cap survives (-12% -> -8%) — energy that passes through the bubble comes back smoother, redistributed along u rather than at the gradient peak. Gamma's volume grows with Re, so the bubble is not a shrinking self-similar layer (a Leray-type recursion would shrink; Necas–Ruzicka–Sverak rule that out anyway).

**3.8 Chains and ends.** A 3-wavelength Crow perturbation nucleates three bubbles at 2 pi/3 spacing, each a mirror pair on the two tubes. With the exact identity -u.grad Pi = -div(Pi u), each bubble's exchange is the inward flux of Pi u through its boundary, split by face. In the periodic chain every bubble is two-sided (face symmetry .62–1.0). In a finite chain (envelope-localised perturbation, free ends) the centre bubble is two-sided to three digits and the flanking bubbles are single-sided (symmetry .00–.33; +.497 through the face toward the chain vs -.035 away), in exact mirror pairs. The zero-divisor channels (P = w.S.w ~ 0 with live w, S) are anti-correlated with the bubbles along the line while the chain forms (-.35 to -.71) — they run between the bubbles, which is the earlier "never on a channel" result seen from the other side.

## 4. What is established, what was falsified, what is open

Established within the model, on two unrelated flow families, with grid and dt checks: the interface forms on the gradient concentration; the load is geometrically locked, pressure-orthogonal, third-ring-anchored, and vortical; the sink is energy-consistent and its effect grows with load and with Re over the range reached; the interface can be two-sided; it chains along a line with single-sided ends.

Falsified: the published three-factor theta as a mechanism that strengthens with gradients (contrast collapse); frame geometry as the nucleation rule; a material or Eulerian field with tau slower than the load's residence time; "interface on the zero-divisor channels".

Open, and in the theory's hands: the magnitude-plane weight (0.4 never fires, 0.96 always fires); tau as a function of U/h; kappa and beta_b; a thin-interface rule at high Re; a bubble with its own dynamics (sub-grid energy equation); a proof-shaped statement — the Ladyzhenskaya route needs a sink growing faster than |grad u|^{2.5}, and the only such scaling measured (grad^3.0) was on the forced ring and did not transfer.

Not done, and it should be said plainly: no run here is at a Reynolds number where Navier–Stokes is hard; nothing here bears on the Earth–Moon claims of the paper; and the reweighted theta is a modification of the published one.

## 5. The one next experiment

State the mechanism's numbers first — weight, tau(U/h), kappa, the interface threshold, the predicted reduction of peak enstrophy and vorticity, the predicted Gamma volume — then run Form D against standard initial data the field already uses (Kerr's antiparallel tubes with published parameters, or Taylor–Green) at 128^3 or better, ideally natively on the desktop overnight. That is the difference between "consistent with" and "predicts".
